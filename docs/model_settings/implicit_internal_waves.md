# Implicit internal waves (IMEX)

In the standard baroclinic step every term is explicit, so on most realistic domains the time step `dt` is limited by the fastest internal gravity waves (mode 1, a few m/s in deep stratified water). The CPP key `IMEX_IGW` makes the **internal-wave terms implicit**: the baroclinic pressure gradient and the buoyancy response to the transport divergence get implicit weights in both stages of the LF-AM3 step (an implicit-explicit, IMEX, step). After each explicit stage one elliptic problem is solved for the implicit correction, by a preconditioned conjugate-gradient method (by default the mode-split preconditioner, which is a Jacobi preconditioner where it would not pay; `imex_pc`), and the momentum and the tracer transport of the stage are corrected. Advection, Coriolis, mixing and forcing stay explicit.

The step is then stable for internal-wave Courant numbers well above 1, and **`dt` is limited by horizontal advection** instead (section 3). Without `IMEX_IGW` the code is unchanged (the namelist then sets `imex_igw = .false.`).

## 1. When to use it

- **Domains whose step is set by internal waves.** Nested regional domains with steep topography and strong stratification, whose stock step is short compared with the advective limit, gain most. Five nests ran at 3 to 10 times their stock step (section 3).
- **Cost.** One IMEX step costs 1.5 to 1.9 times a stock step (the two elliptic solves, the reference stratification and the operator; the solver needs 1-4 iterations per stage on a Pacific basin, more on steep nests and at longer steps). The gain in computing time (without reading forcing files) was 6.3 times on a 1.25 km Iceland nest (600 s against 60 s), 3.2 times on a 1 km Hawaii nest (900 s against 150 s) and 1.3 times on a Pacific basin (1800 s against 900 s). Reading surface forcing and boundary files is paid per model hour whatever the step; on the nests it took 53-70 % of the IMEX runs' wall time, which made the wall-time gain 2.9 and 1.6 times.
- **Domains already near their advective limit gain little.** The Pacific basin's stock step is within a factor of two of its advective limit.

The IMEX step gives different answers from the explicit step: it damps and slows internal waves whose frequency is not resolved by `dt`, as implicit schemes do. On the nests the error of the internal tide against the parent model did not grow from the stock step to the long step; the barotropic tide is set by the free-surface scheme and its error grows with `dt` (section 3).

## 2. How to turn it on

1. Add `#define IMEX_IGW` to the case's `cppdefs.opt` (it is not compatible with the non-hydrostatic solver, `nhmg = .true.` in `NHMG_SETTINGS`).
2. Copy the namelist group `IMEX_IGW_SETTINGS` from `$ROMS_ROOT/src/namelist.nml` and set `imex_igw = .true.`. Every namelist has this group: `imex_igw` is required and must match the build (`.true.` with `IMEX_IGW`, `.false.` without), and a run without the group or the key, or with a value that does not match the build, stops at startup. The template's other values are the defaults and the recommended settings.
3. Use the implicit free surface (`fs_mode = 'implicit'` in `IMPLICIT_FS`, with its default settings). IMEX also runs with the split-explicit fast loop, but that loop's cost grows with `ndtfast`, which must grow with `dt`. The implicit free surface's defaults are the tested combination with IMEX (Pacific basin at 1440-2400 s, the nests); no IMEX-specific free-surface setting is needed. See [Implicit free surface](implicit_free_surface.md).
4. Choose `dt` (section 3).

The startup log prints the settings and the derived weights (`IMEX_IGW: on (imex_igw = .true.), implicit internal-wave terms`). A run without IMEX prints `IMEX_IGW: off (imex_igw = .false.)`.

To switch IMEX off again, set `imex_igw = .false.` and rebuild without `IMEX_IGW`; the group's other keys can stay (they are ignored, with a startup note).

## 3. Choosing the time step

The limit is explicit horizontal advection: in the strongest currents a grid-scale wave grows once the advective Courant number is too large. Measured on the Sargasso Sea and on five nests, runs fail once the **horizontal advective Courant number** in the strongest currents reaches about 0.85-1.2 (0.8-1.0 on one nest). Choose `dt` so that

    dt (|u|/dx + |v|/dy) <= 0.8

over the whole run at the strongest currents of the domain (the outward-flux form of the Courant number that ROMS reports). The step lines report the largest total Courant number (`MAX_ADV_CFL`) and its vertical part at the same cell; total minus vertical there is a guide to the horizontal part, not its domain maximum. The open boundary and the solver settings do not move this limit; explicit lateral momentum viscosity moves it a little (on the Sargasso Sea, 100 m2/s at 3 km kept 2400 s stable for 60 steps, about 10 % beyond the step that ran without it).

Measured steps (stable runs; the stock step is the explicit model's working step). They were measured with `imex_corr_adv = 0`; the default 1 (section 4) raised the longest stable step where an interior jet set the limit (7-day runs, last stable step: Hawaii nest 1080 s -> 1230 s, Gulf of Mexico nest 600 s -> 660 s, Sargasso Sea from a developed state 2320 s -> 2800 s), after which an open boundary set it on the Sargasso Sea and the Hawaii nest:

| domain | stock step | IMEX step | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iceland nest, 1.25 km | 60 s | 600 s (10x) | 29 days |
| California Current nest | 150 s | 900-1500 s (6-10x) | 10 days |
| Hawaii nest, 1 km | 150 s | 900 s (6x) | 29 days; 1200 s failed on day 2.4 (interior jet) |
| Bay of Bengal nest, 1.5 km | 200 s | 600 s (3x) | 29 days; 900 s failed on the shallow shelf |
| eastern Gulf of Mexico nest, 1.5 km | 200 s | 400 s (2x) | 29 days; at 600 s one run failed on day 26.6 (Loop Current), another completed |
| Sargasso Sea, 3 km | 480 s | 1440-2160 s (3-4.5x) | 2160 s ran the 2 days of forcing available; 2400 s failed in the jet core (horizontal Courant number 0.85-0.95) |
| Pacific basin (1856 x 960 x 100) | 900 s | 1800 s (2x) | 15 days; 2400 s also stable |

The barotropic tide error grows with `dt` (about as `dt^2` in amplitude): on the Pacific basin the M2 sea-level error against a split run at 900 s was 0.79 / 1.19 / 2.04 cm at 1440 / 1800 / 2400 s (signal 31.8 cm rms). Where the barotropic tide matters, prefer the shorter of two stable steps.

- **`imex_bref_hours`** (24). Use 12 where the stratification changes strongly within a day (the 24-h rebuild always falls at the same time of day). It is the longest age of `b_ref`: where `b_ref` goes stale earlier, `imex_bref_trigger` rebuilds it on demand (see [stale reference](#stale-reference-and-imex_bref_trigger)).
- **`imex_obc_mode`** (`'auto'`) and **`imex_obc_tref`** (259200 s = 3 days): the open boundary, see section 5.
- **`imex_rst_state`** (1). With `EXACT_RESTART` (the default), 1 writes the IMEX state into the restart files (about eleven 3-D variables, one copy per file), and a restarted run reproduces the continuous run bit for bit. 0 writes none: much smaller restart files, but the first step after a restart starts the IMEX stores as on the first step of a run, so the restart is not exact.

### Stiff cells and `imex_corr_adv_clip`

The operator is built from the reference stratification `b_ref`, which is smoothed along geopotentials. A neighbour column that does not reach a cell's depth is left out of that smoothing, so near the bed and the surface of sloping columns at a front `b_ref` can differ along a model level by far more than its vertical stratification allows, and the operator's N² of a cell can fall to its floor (`imex_n2_ref`) while the water is stratified. Such a cell is stiff along its level: the implicit correction turns a buoyancy change in the cell into a velocity through its faces on that one level. With `imex_corr_adv = 1` the corrector's advective buoyancy change is such a source. On the Bay of Bengal nest (1.5 km) at 780-840 s this drove single-level currents of 1-25 m/s in 40-55 cells per 7-day run, most in water shallower than 30 m, and 2 of 4 runs blew up at 810 s and at 840 s.

`imex_corr_adv_clip` caps that change per cell, so a stiff cell behaves as with `imex_corr_adv = 0`. With the default 0.1, all 7-day runs on that nest at 810 s and 840 s completed (4 of 4 at each) and no velocity correction exceeded 0.64 m/s (0.38 m/s with `imex_corr_adv = 0`); the clip acted on 1e-5 of the cell-steps. In 15-day runs at 780 s the clipped run failed on day 11.4 at the same step as the run with `imex_corr_adv = 0`, in a river-plume jet (an advective limit, section 3); without the clip it failed on day 8.7 in stiff cells. It did not act on the Hawaii nest at 1290 s or on the Sargasso Sea at 1440 s, whose results are unchanged bit for bit. The clip does not repair `b_ref`: the stiff cells remain, with the behaviour they have without `imex_corr_adv`. With `imex_verbose >= 1` an `IMEX_CLIP` line at each rebuild and at the end of the run counts the cell-steps whose change was clipped.

### Vertical Courant number above 1 and `imex_corr_adv_vimp`

The buoyancy change that `imex_corr_adv = 1` adds is estimated with the tracer corrector's advection taken explicitly. The tracer step itself advects the vertical velocity in two parts: `We` explicitly, and `Wi`, the part above the explicit scheme's limit, implicitly. Where the vertical Courant number `dt pm pn |We + Wi| / min(Hz)` exceeds 1 at a cell's top or bottom face, on any step and in any cell, the explicit estimate overshoots: on the Sargasso Sea an open-edge corner with vertical Courant numbers of 3-8 at the start of a run (2240 s, `imex_mix_split = 1`) blew up within 7 steps with it. Such cells are few but present at most steps: about 60 per step on the Sargasso Sea at 2880 s and 200 on the Gulf of Mexico nest at 675 s (1e-5 to 4e-5 of the cells), 0.05 % of the cells on the Pacific basin at 1800 s and 0.26 % on the Bay of Bengal nest at 780 s, where a third of them change state from one step to the next.

With the default `imex_corr_adv_vimp = 0` such a cell gets no change and runs as with `imex_corr_adv = 0`. With 1 it gets the change from the tracer step's own estimate: the buoyancy at level n advanced by the explicit part (horizontal advection and `We`), then by the implicit first-order upwind step with `Wi`, one tridiagonal solve per column that has such a cell. The estimated buoyancy stays within the range of the column's values, but the change itself is not bounded at large Courant numbers, by design: it is measured against the linear response that the implicit solve adds, which grows with the Courant number, and takes the excess out again.

In tests 1 behaved as well as 0 or better: the Sargasso Sea corner case above completes with both, with 1 at a smaller largest velocity correction (0.077 against 0.128 m/s); every longest stable step that was tried held with 1 (Sargasso Sea 2880 s and 3200 s, Hawaii nest 900 s and 1290 s, Bay of Bengal nest 780 s, Gulf of Mexico nest 675 s, Pacific basin 1800 s), and none moved (the Gulf of Mexico nest fails at 690 s with both, in the Loop Current). The results change only where such cells occur: on the Hawaii nest at 900 s they are identical up to the first step that has one, and on the Sargasso Sea at 2880 s the change is confined to the one site that has them, where 1 is 3-4 % closer to a run at 720 s. The cost is below the run-to-run spread: on the Gulf of Mexico nest at 675 s and on the Pacific basin at 1800 s (about 60 000 such cells per step) the time per step with 1 is within 0.3 % of that with 0. Swept-volume vertical advection, whose long steps keep cells over a shelf break at vertical Courant numbers of 1-3, needs 1: with 0 the cells there switch between the change and none from step to step, and the switching grew until the run stopped. With `imex_verbose = 2` and 1 an `IMEX_VIMP` line per step counts the cells that took the estimate.

### Stale reference and `imex_bref_trigger`

Between rebuilds the operator holds the stratification of `b_ref`, and the step carries the difference between the present stratification and that of `b_ref` explicitly. Where a mixed layer deepens within hours this difference grows, and at long steps a grid-scale mode of the stiff internal waves can grow out of it. In a development configuration of the Gulf of Mexico nest (1.5 km) at 1350 s with `imex_theta_c = 0.7`, the run with the 24-h cadence died on day 6.7 at such a site, and with rebuilds on demand it ran 29 days. At the step lengths of this release the mode has not limited a run (the nest held 29 days at 600 s with `b_ref` never rebuilt), but the exposure reaches the same level on storm days.

Every step the model measures, per column, the exposure `E`: for each of the three fastest vertical modes of the operator's stratification (WKB modes of its N²), the part of N²(present) - N²(`b_ref`) that projects on the mode, divided by the operator's N² on the mode, and weighted by `W²/(W² + 2)`, where `W = c dt sqrt(4 pm² + 4 pn²)` is the mode's internal-wave Courant number for the grid-scale wave (so modes that the step resolves count little); this is the raw exposure `e`. `E` is its largest value over the modes divided by `x_inf`, the value at which a one-mode model of the step goes unstable for stiff waves with the time-step weights in effect (0.310 for the default weights, 0.270 with `imex_theta_c = 0.7`, 1/(2 `imex_theta_c`) with `imex_corr_adv = 0`; printed at startup). `E` is thus a fraction of a linear limit, and one value of `imex_bref_trigger` serves any weights. Columns within two cells of an open edge are left out: their stratification is set by the boundary values within a step.

When `E` exceeds `imex_bref_trigger` (default 0.646, which is `e = 0.2` with the default weights) in some column and `b_ref` is at least 1 h old, `b_ref`, the operator and its preconditioner are rebuilt at the next step; `imex_bref_hours` remains the longest age. The decision uses the domain maximum of `E`, which is the same on any tiling, and the age of `b_ref`, which is in the restart file, so restarts stay exact. In 7-day runs it rebuilt `b_ref` 6 times on the Gulf of Mexico nest at 600 s (on days 5.4-7, at ages of 6-10 h) and 3 times in 3 days on the Sargasso Sea at 2880 s; it never acted on the Hawaii nest at 900 s, whose internal tide takes `E` to 0.43, and those results are unchanged bit for bit. Computing `E` costs about 0.5 % of the step (0.4-0.6 ms of 113-119 ms per step on these nests on one node); a rebuild costs about a quarter of a step (28 ms on the Gulf of Mexico nest).

The log reports `E` in every run, at every `imex_verbose`: an `IMEX_BREF_DEMAND` line at each demand; at each rebuild an `IMEX_BREF_E` line with its reason (`cadence` or `demand`), the age of `b_ref` and the largest `E` since the last rebuild (with `e`, the step, the age, the column, the mode, the mode's residual `x_m` and `W`); and two `IMEX_BREF_E` lines at the end of the run (the largest `E` of the run, and the counts of rebuilds and demands). With `imex_verbose >= 1` an `IMEX_BREF_E` line follows each `IMEX` line. A run that sets `imex_bref_trigger = 0` never rebuilds on demand and still reports `E`, so the lines show how close a run comes to the trigger.

## 5. Open boundaries

`imex_obc_mode` selects how the normal velocity at the open-boundary faces enters the IMEX step:

- **`'fixed'`**: the open faces are outside the implicit operator; their normal velocity is the explicit value of the boundary conditions (`obc_m3`, ...), as without IMEX, and enters each stage's right-hand side.
- **`'modal'`**: a characteristic (per-mode Flather) condition on the internal modes, implicit in the stage solves. At each open face column the baroclinic normal velocity is `u_b = u_ext + s Y (p_int - p_ext - m)`: the boundary data's velocity plus the radiation of the baroclinic pressure mismatch between the interior and the boundary data, with the admittance `Y` built from the vertical modes of the operator's stratification in that column (the first `imex_obc_nmodes` modes with `1/c_m`, the rest with the last mode's admittance). Outgoing internal waves leave and the internal tide that the boundary data carry comes in. `m` is a slow reference of the mismatch (time scale `imex_obc_tref`), so a slow child-parent mismatch is held instead of being radiated as a flow through the boundary. The barotropic mode and the tracers keep their usual boundary conditions.
- **`'auto'`** (default): `'modal'` where it is supported (an open edge, `use_m3_boundary_data` and `use_t_boundary_data`), else `'fixed'`; the startup log prints the choice.

On the nests whose boundary data carry the parent's internal tide, `'modal'` admitted 75-100 % of the parent's incoming mode-1 tide near the edges, against 20-70 % with the stock boundary, and a Pacific basin (boundary data without internal tide) ran 21 days at 1440 s with quiet edges. It costs at most 0.4 % of the step (the modes are recomputed at each rebuild of `b_ref`).

Recommended use of `'modal'`:

- **Start.** A run that does not start from the state its boundary data come from (a nest not initialised from its parent) should take its first ~10 days with `imex_obc_mode = 'fixed'` and switch to `'modal'` at a restart. When the reference is set to the present mismatch (a start without it, or a change of the boundary settings at a restart) the log prints, per open edge, the rms of the held mismatch and of the face velocity it would drive, and a warning for a gross mismatch (for example child depths that differ from the parent's along an edge).
- **Sponge.** A nest whose boundary data carry the internal tide runs best with a quarter of the `v_sponge` used with the stock boundary (the sponge damps the incoming tide); an outermost domain, whose boundary data have no internal tide, keeps the stock value. With the default `imex_mix_split = 2` the sponge can keep its usual strength (`v_sponge` = grid spacing in m / 10, a `visc2 dt/dx^2` of `dt/(10 dx)`): the Sargasso Sea ran 7 days at 3200 s with `v_sponge` 300 (0.11), and nests off Iceland, Hawaii and in the Bay of Bengal ran 29 days with it at 600-900 s. Keep `visc2 dt/dx^2` below the explicit Laplacian's own limit, about 0.25 (a note is printed above 0.2). With `imex_mix_split = 1` keep it at or below 0.05 (a note is printed above 0.1; on a Hawaii nest 0.17 blew up and 0.085 ran 29 days, on the Sargasso Sea 0.093 failed after 2-3.5 days at an open edge and 0.097 at 2880 s after 4.2 days, where 2 runs 7 days).
- **`apply_sponge_tune`** has no effect on the normal velocity under `'modal'`: leave it off (a note is printed).
- **`imex_obc_tref`** (3 days): choose at least about three inertial periods at the lowest `|f|` of the open edges, and not less than 1 day. 3 days covers edges poleward of about 30 degrees; at 20 degrees take about 4.5 days, at 10 degrees about 9 days. The first rebuild prints the filter's response at that inertial frequency against the rule's 0.053. A reference that follows the mismatch within hours stops the radiation of edge transients (on a Hawaii nest 6 h blew up within a day). 0 switches the reference off, so the whole mismatch is radiated.
- **`imex_obc_q0`** (code-only, 0 = off). Rotating tuning of the admittances, `y_m = 1/(q0 c_m)`, exact at normal incidence for one frequency `omega0`: `q0 = sqrt(1 - (f/omega0)^2)`, with `omega0` usually M2 and `f` taken at the open edges where the internal tide enters. Set it only where `f/omega0 > 0.6` there (poleward of about 35 degrees for M2); otherwise leave 0. Allowed values: 0 or 0.2 to 1. The cost: a wave of another frequency is reflected by `(q0 - q)/(q0 + q)` in amplitude, `q = sqrt(1 - (f/omega)^2)`, and part of a Kelvin wave leaving along a coast is reflected (13 % at `q0` = 0.5). On the nests that used it the tuning gave no measurable gain.

Known limits of `'modal'`: waves that the boundary data do not contain and that leave at more than about 60 degrees from the normal return at the rate of a one-direction condition (36 % at 75 degrees, 50 % at 80 degrees in a channel test), and the sponge does not change that; the child's depths must match the parent's along the open edges.

## 6. Restarts

With `imex_rst_state = 1` the restart file holds the stored pressure gradient, the memory term, the last solutions of both stages (the warm start), the last reference stratification and the layer thicknesses of the last rebuild, and a record of scalars (`imex_rst_scal`). With `'modal'` it also holds the boundary reference `m` (`imex_obcm`). A restarted run reproduces the continuous run bit for bit, also when the restart falls between two rebuilds. This needs reproducible global sums: where `MPI_Allreduce` is not reproducible across runs (for example conda-forge MPICH with its device collectives), set `imex_redu = 1`. Without it the model fields are unaffected, but the IMEX solver state in the restart file can differ in the last bit, so the restarted run may not match the continuous one bit for bit. A run can start from a file without the IMEX state (an initial file, a restart written without `IMEX_IGW` or with `imex_rst_state = 0`); a change of the solver settings, of `imex_bref_hours` or of the open-boundary settings across a restart is allowed. In those cases the IMEX stores start as on a first step (and the `'modal'` reference from the present mismatch) and the log says that the restart is not exact.

## 7. Limitations

- Not compatible with the non-hydrostatic solver (`nhmg = .true.` in `NHMG_SETTINGS`): ROMS stops at startup.
- Horizontal advection stays explicit and limits `dt` (section 3).
- Lateral tracer mixing is taken from the state at level n.
- Results depend on the tiling at the level of the solver tolerances (and of the implicit free surface's solve); `imex_redu = 1` removes the IMEX solves' part where the preconditioner is Jacobi (`imex_pc = 'jacobi'`, or the default `'modes'` where its automatic choice keeps no mode). Where `'modes'` keeps modes, its V-cycle works inside each tile (two tilings agree to the tolerance level; at 1e-12 to rounding).
- The solver's iteration count grows with `dt` and differs between domains (1-4 iterations per stage on the Pacific basin at 1440-2400 s, 7-16 on the Hawaii nest at 900 s).
- Tested in CI (`tests/test_imex_igw.py`, the suite's small open-boundary grid on 4 ranks, with the implicit free surface and the tidal potential): the output and an exact restart across a rebuild of the operator with the `'fixed'` and the `'modal'` boundary, `imex_corr_adv_vimp = 1` (read, and equal to 0 where no cell exceeds a vertical Courant number of 1: the CI grid has none, so the estimate itself is tested outside CI), and the removed-key stop with the template's `sed` line; and on the suite's MARBL case with BGC tracer subcycling (`bgc_physics_subcycle`) and both boundaries: the window budgets, an exact restart at a window boundary inside a rebuild period, and physics output equal to the run without subcycling. Not tested in CI: closed and periodic domains, `mpi_masking` and `parallel_io`. Outside CI IMEX has run on a closed basin with a river and a CDR release, the Sargasso Sea, five nests and a Pacific basin.

Settings: see [`IMEX_IGW_SETTINGS`](runtime_settings.md#imex_igw_settings) in the runtime settings.
